\section{Precision}
\label{sec:precision}

\begin{question}
  \textbf{What do FP8 and MXFP8 buy, and do they train the same?} Low precision doubles the matmul rate but not the network: does the gain measured on one node hold at scale? Does the loss follow BF16, within its noise?
\end{question}

\paragraph{Speed (C-speed), answer.} Little: MXFP8 speeds up Qwen by $\times$\TriQwenFpOne{} on 1 node and $\times$\TriQwenFpTop{} on \TriQwenFpTopNodes{} nodes (by mean step, $\times$\TriQwenFpOneMean{} and $\times$\TriQwenFpTopMean), and GLM-5 by $\times$\TriGlmFpMx{} at 13 nodes and $\times$\TriGlmFpMxTop{} at \TriGlmFpTopNodes, against nearly $\times$2 on a matrix multiply alone. FP8 is really in use. Matrix multiplies are just too small a share of a step. The gain holds with scale, up to \TriQwenFpTopNodes{} nodes on Qwen and \TriGlmFpTopNodes{} on GLM-5, across the bandwidth groups. Qwen's step times here are throttled by the CPU clock drop (\cref{sec:machine}); GLM-5's are not.

\paragraph{What was measured.} BF16 and MXFP8 for Qwen3.8-27B at \texttt{hsdp8} and 24k tokens per GPU, on the \texttt{torchtitan} stacked layout, which MXFP8 needs (the pinned TorchTitan has no other FP8 recipe); BF16, FP8 tensorwise, FP8 blockwise and MXFP8 for GLM-5 at \texttt{pp13-ep8}, with batch 256 at 13 nodes and batch 768, three copies, at \TriGlmFpTopNodes{} (\cref{fig:precision_speed}). Qwen ran three times at 1 node, with BF16 runs \TriQwenFpOneSpread\,\% apart, as large as the gain itself. GLM-5 has one run per recipe at each size, over 19 steps, and these runs cannot rank its recipes: at \TriGlmFpTopNodes{} nodes, tensorwise, blockwise and MXFP8 give $\times$\TriGlmFpTensorTop, $\times$\TriGlmFpBlockTop{} and $\times$\TriGlmFpMxTop{} by median, and $\times$\TriGlmFpTensorTopMean, $\times$\TriGlmFpBlockTopMean{} and $\times$\TriGlmFpMxTopMean{} by mean step, leaving out the slow first measured step. The other Qwen runs use the \texttt{rows} stacked layout, and BF16 on \texttt{hsdp8} is $\times$\TriStackedGain{} on \texttt{torchtitan}, so compare these two recipes with each other, not with the numbers of \cref{sec:layouts}.

\textbf{FP8 is real.} A standalone $8192^2$ matmul on B200 reaches 2,991--3,053\,TFLOP/s in FP8 and 1,580--1,637 in BF16, with the math library's log naming the 8-bit type. In GLM-5, the same logs confirm FP8 for tensorwise and blockwise: 97\,\% of matrix-multiply FLOPs on two of the three ranks logged, 76\,\% on the third, whose output layer stays in BF16. On B200, blockwise runs as MXFP8 matrix multiplies. MXFP8 itself is identified only from kernel names, because its library logs were lost.

\textbf{Where the gain goes.} One profiled GLM-5 step, on one of the 3 ranks logged: matrix multiplies take 6.25\,s of about 48\,s in BF16, and 4.28\,s with quantization in MXFP8, a saving of 2.0\,s, which is most of the 2.64\,s between the two recipes' mean steps. The rest does not change: 12--20\,s in NCCL and 22--30\,s in other kernels, across those ranks. That is one step on three ranks, so it explains the size of the gain but is not a measurement of it.

\textbf{Qwen depends on recompute.} Under full recompute, MXFP8 gives $\times$\TriQwenFpFull{} on 1 node, against $\times$\TriQwenFpOne{} under selective. A likely reason (inferred): the selective recompute list saves no \texttt{\_scaled\_mm} output, so MXFP8 recomputes its matrix multiplies while BF16 keeps them. Adding them to the list ran out of memory.

\textbf{GLM-5 over a longer run.} Over 500 steps, BF16 steps average \TriGlmLongStepBf\,s and MXFP8 \TriGlmLongStepMx\,s (medians, $\times$\TriGlmLongFp), against \TriGlmStepBf\,s for BF16 in the 19-step run. The BF16 and MXFP8 runs were on different nodes; a second BF16 run, on MXFP8's nodes, gives \TriGlmLongRepStep\,s and the same $\times$\TriGlmLongFpSame, and a third there, with 2 compile workers per rank instead of 32 (\cref{sec:machine}), \TriGlmLongThirdStep\,s. FP8 blockwise and tensorwise, on other nodes, take \TriGlmLongBlockStep\,s and \TriGlmLongTensorStep\,s ($\times$\TriGlmLongFpBlock{} and $\times$\TriGlmLongFpTensor). The speed is not steady. BF16 slows down after step 3, once the learning rate starts to move the weights, and its memory rises to \TriGlmLongMemBf\,GiB; it holds at about \TriGlmLongBfMid\,s until about step 350, then speeds up to \TriGlmLongBfEnd\,s by step 500. All the runs at this seed do this, from about step 300--350, on different sets of nodes and on different days: the second BF16 goes from about \TriGlmLongRepMid\,s to \TriGlmLongRepEnd\,s, the third from \TriGlmLongThirdMid\,s to \TriGlmLongThirdEnd\,s, MXFP8 from \TriGlmLongMxMid\,s to \TriGlmLongMxEnd\,s, blockwise from \TriGlmLongBlockMid\,s to \TriGlmLongBlockEnd\,s, and tensorwise from \TriGlmLongTensorMid\,s to \TriGlmLongTensorEnd\,s. BF16 at another seed, on other nodes, starts earlier, at about step 200, and goes from about \TriGlmLongSeedMid\,s to \TriGlmLongSeedEnd\,s. So the speed-up follows the step and the seed, not the hour or the nodes. The third BF16 run is faster than the second up to step 350 and slower from step 400, so fewer compile workers show no clear effect on speed. BF16 gains the most from it, so on the same nodes MXFP8's gain is down to $\times$\TriGlmLongFpSameEnd{} by the end. The cause is not measured.

\textbf{Memory.} Qwen's peak falls from \TriQwenMemBf{} to \TriQwenMemMx\,GiB with MXFP8 under selective recompute, but that comes from selective recompute, not from FP8: with full recompute both peak at \TriQwenFullMem\,GiB. GLM-5's peak rises with FP8, from \TriGlmMemBf{} to \TriGlmMemTensor--\TriGlmMemMx\,GiB (+\TriGlmMemPctTensor\ to +\TriGlmMemPctMx\,\%) at 13 nodes and from \TriGlmMemBfTop{} to \TriGlmMemTensorTop--\TriGlmMemMxTop\,GiB (+\TriGlmMemPctTensorTop\ to +\TriGlmMemPctMxTop\,\%) at \TriGlmFpTopNodes, and we do not know why. Tensorwise adds the least at both sizes.

\paragraph{Loss (C-loss), answer.} \textbf{Not settled.} Whether MXFP8 trains the same as BF16 depends so far on the seed, and 500 steps are too few to say which seed is typical. On Qwen, MXFP8 ends \TriLossSeedOneLo{} to \TriLossSeedOneHi{} above BF16 at seed 1234, and \TriLossSeedTwoLo{} and \TriLossSeedThreeLo{} at seeds 4321 and 5678 (mean loss over steps 401--500). On GLM-5, MXFP8 ends \TriLossGlmLast{} and \TriLossGlmMxThirdLast{} from two BF16 runs on its own nodes, the third \TriLossGlmThirdRepLast{} from the second. Early on, over steps 51--100, it was \TriLossGlmMxEarly{} from them while they agreed within 0.01. The larger gaps come with a change of nodes: those two BF16 runs end \TriLossGlmSameLast{} and \TriLossGlmThirdFirstLast{} above a BF16 run at the same seed on another set. Blockwise and tensorwise ran only on other nodes, so their gaps mix the recipe with the nodes. Each FP8 recipe has one run, so this does not show that the recipes train alike.

\paragraph{What was measured.} The recipes train for 500 steps at 13 nodes on the same FineWeb data with a realistic learning rate, at \texttt{hsdp8} and 24k tokens per GPU for Qwen, \texttt{pp13-ep8} for GLM-5. The loss is logged at each step and runs are compared on that index (\cref{fig:precision_loss,tab:loss_gaps}). On Qwen, a seed sets the data order as well as the initialization, so the seeds differ in what the model reads. On GLM-5 the second seed does not reshuffle the data. GLM-5's runs are on different sets of 13 nodes: BF16 at seed 1234 on \texttt{trig0114--0121} with \texttt{0124--0128}, and twice on \texttt{trig0101--0113}, where MXFP8 also ran; BF16 at seed 4321 on the set in the second bandwidth group; FP8 blockwise on the 13 nodes returned or fixed on November~6; FP8 tensorwise on the first of these sets with \texttt{trig0136} in place of \texttt{trig0114}.

\textbf{Against the noise, on Qwen.} Two runs at the same seed differ by at most \TriLossSameMax, so the seed 1234 gap is well above that noise. It is not above the difference between seeds: BF16 at seed 1234 ends \TriLossOtherMax{} below BF16 at the other two seeds, while MXFP8 at all three seeds ends within \TriLossOtherMxMax{} of each other. Most of the gap at seed 1234 therefore comes from that one BF16 trajectory; the nodes do not explain it, since the crossed runs give the same gap on both sets.

\textbf{What the curves share.} In all three seeds MXFP8 is ahead early, then the gap rises at a similar rate until the end, passing zero at about step 250--300 at seed 1234 and only at about step 475--500 at the other two seeds (\cref{fig:precision_loss}). At step 500 the gaps are \TriLossSeedOneEnd, \TriLossSeedTwoEnd{} and \TriLossSeedThreeEnd. If that rise continued, MXFP8 would end behind at every seed; that is an extrapolation, and 500 steps cannot tell. A longer run would.

\textbf{On GLM-5, the loss late in training lines up with the nodes, not the recipe.} All GLM-5 runs give the same loss at steps 1 and 2 and part from step 3 (by 0.0003 to 0.0015 between BF16 runs), so even on the same nodes a run does not repeat exactly. Of the two BF16 runs at seed 1234 on \texttt{trig0101--0113}, the third ends \TriLossGlmThirdRepLast{} from the second (\TriLossGlmThirdRepEnd{} at step 500), after being up to 0.10 apart over steps 201--300, and MXFP8 on the same nodes ends \TriLossGlmLast{} and \TriLossGlmMxThirdLast{} from them (\TriLossGlmEnd{} and \TriLossGlmMxThirdEnd{} at step 500). Those two BF16 runs end \TriLossGlmSameLast{} and \TriLossGlmThirdFirstLast{} above the first, on \texttt{trig0114--0121} with \texttt{0124--0128} (\TriLossGlmSameEnd{} and \TriLossGlmThirdFirstEnd{} at step 500). Tensorwise, on 12 of the first run's 13 nodes, ends \TriLossGlmTensorLast{} from it (\TriLossGlmTensorEnd{} at step 500) and \TriLossGlmTensorRep{} from the second BF16. Blockwise, on the returned and fixed nodes, falls between: \TriLossGlmBlockLast{} from the first BF16 and \TriLossGlmBlockRep{} from the second. The grouping does not follow the kernel change of November~5 (\cref{sec:machine}): MXFP8 ran before it and ends with the two BF16 runs after it, and tensorwise ran after it and ends with the BF16 run before it. Early on the runs group otherwise: over steps 51--100 the two BF16 runs on \texttt{trig0101--0113} and tensorwise are 0.28--0.30 above the first BF16, against +0.03 for MXFP8 and +0.09 for blockwise. Inferred: something that comes with the set of nodes moves GLM-5's loss by about 0.25 by step 500, more than MXFP8's gap on the same nodes or tensorwise's on nearly the same. With one to three runs per set, chance is not ruled out, and the cause is not measured. BF16 at another seed, on a third set, ends \TriLossGlmOtherLast{} above the first (\TriLossGlmOtherEnd). GLM-5's learning rate starts at 0 and warms up over 50 steps, so its first steps say little about a recipe.

\textbf{Missing on GLM-5:} a second run of each FP8 recipe, BF16 on the nodes where blockwise ran, and runs of one recipe on more sets of nodes, to test whether the loss follows the nodes.
